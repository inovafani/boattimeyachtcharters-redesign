You have the ring, and you have decided the question should be asked on the
water. It is a good setting for it: the light does half the work, and there is
nowhere else either of you needs to be. It also runs to a timetable you do not
control, so the planning is mostly about a few decisions made in the right
order.

There are two ways to do it. If you want the boat to yourselves, the page that
takes the enquiry is [private yacht charter on the Gold Coast](/private-yacht-charter).
If two seats at sunset is closer to what you have in mind, that is the
[Luxury Scenic Sunset Cruise](/luxury-broadwater-cruise). The rest of this guide
helps you choose between them and plan around the one you pick.

## 1. Decide whether you want an audience

This is the first decision, and it is really a question about your partner
rather than about boats.

- **A private charter** means the vessel carries your group and nobody else.
  You choose the time, the route and who is on board, whether that is the two
  of you or both families waiting out of sight.
- **The Luxury Scenic Sunset Cruise** is booked seat by seat on Sun Goddess, so
  you share the boat with other guests. It runs for two and a half hours on
  the Broadwater, leaving at five o'clock, and tickets include a nibbles share
  plate for two with a licensed bar on board.

Some people would love a deck of strangers cheering. Others would rather the
moment belonged to two people. If you are not sure which your partner is, plan
for the quieter version.

## 2. Work back from the light

Most people picture the question being asked at sunset. That gives you a
fixed point to plan from.

Look up the sunset time for your date, then work backwards. On the scheduled
sunset cruise the departure time is set, so check how the two line up before
you book. On a private charter the timing is yours to arrange, and it is worth
asking for a departure that puts you on the water well before the light goes
rather than racing it.

Ask earlier than feels necessary. If the ring comes out as the colour starts,
you have the rest of the sunset to enjoy being engaged, and the photos are
taken in good light instead of the last of it.

## 3. Tell the right people beforehand

A proposal is a surprise for one person. Everybody else who can help should
know.

- **Tell the operator when you enquire.** Say that it is a proposal, when you
  would like to ask, and whether you want it kept quiet. Ask what the crew can
  do to help on the day.
- **Ask about a photographer before you book one.** If you want the moment
  photographed, raise it with the operator first, so everyone knows who is
  coming aboard and where they can stand.
- **Brief one accomplice.** If family or friends are involved, one person
  should hold the plan and the timings, and it should not be you.

## 4. Check what can come on board

The rule that catches people out: **no outside food or drink is allowed on
board.** That includes the bottle of champagne you were saving for the moment.

It is easily solved, as long as you solve it early. Both vessels have licensed
bars, and on a private charter the catering is arranged ahead through the
operator's culinary partners, from grazing boards and canapés to a private
chef. If you want a particular bottle opened the moment the answer is yes, ask
for it when you arrange the catering.

Flowers, a sign or anything else you plan to bring should be raised at the
same time. Ask rather than assume.

## 5. Plan for the weather you get

You cannot book the sky. Both vessels have covered decks and indoor spaces, so
a grey evening changes the backdrop without cancelling the plan. Decide in
advance where on board you would ask if the open deck is not an option, so you
are not improvising with a ring box in your pocket.

Wind matters more than cloud on the day. Hair, hats and anything loose behave
differently on an open deck, and a ring box is safer in a zipped pocket than
in a jacket draped over a chair.

## 6. Get the arrival right

Boarding is at Marina Mirage Seaworld Drive, Main Beach, about ten minutes from
Surfers Paradise and fifteen from Broadbeach. The boat leaves on schedule, so
build in more time than you think you need. Arriving flustered is a poor
start to the evening you have in mind.

If your partner does not know where you are going, think about how much the
marina gives away, and have a line ready. A sunset cruise for no particular
reason is a perfectly believable plan.

Think about afterwards as well. Once you are back at the marina there will be
phone calls to make, so book the dinner or the drive home before the day, not
on the dock.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can we have the boat to ourselves for a proposal?

Yes. A private charter carries your group and nobody else, and the enquiry
starts on the private yacht charter page. Sun Goddess takes up to 135 guests on
a day charter and Mermaid Spirit up to 150, so a charter also suits a proposal
followed by a party with family and friends.

### Can we propose on the sunset cruise instead?

Yes. The Luxury Scenic Sunset Cruise is booked seat by seat, so other guests
will be on board. It suits a couple who are happy to share the moment, or who
want a quiet corner of the deck and the skyline behind them.

### Can we bring our own champagne?

No outside food or drink is permitted on board. Both vessels have licensed
bars, and on a private charter you can ask for something particular when the
catering is arranged.

### What happens if it rains?

Both vessels have covered decks and indoor spaces, so the evening goes ahead
under cover. Choose a second spot on board in advance, in case the open deck
is wet or windy.

### Will the crew help with the surprise?

Tell the operator it is a proposal when you enquire, and ask what the crew can
do on the day. The earlier they know, the easier it is to plan around.

A private charter takes one group at a time, so the date comes first. Send the
